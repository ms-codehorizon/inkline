Theo, Wedding Planner

Dana: You’ve been planning this wedding for three years.
Theo: And every option is still available. I’m very pleased.
Dana: Have they chosen a date?
Theo: They liked June, but choosing June would eliminate eleven excellent months.
Dana: Usually that’s what choosing does.
Theo: Exactly. I protect couples from that sort of loss.
Dana: Have they booked a venue?
Theo: They have a shortlist of forty-six.
Dana: That sounds like a long list.
Theo: It was eighty. We’ve made some brave decisions.
Dana: Such as?
Theo: Removing venues that had already closed.
(Dana writes.)
Dana: What happens when the couple agree on something?
Theo: I ask whether they’ve considered the alternatives.
Dana: And then?
Theo: They become less certain. That’s where my expertise really helps.
Dana: Surely they want to get married.
Theo: Absolutely. It’s their favourite possibility.
Dana: What are you working on this week?
Theo: The vows. We’re concerned about the word “do.”
Dana: Because it’s a commitment?
Theo: Because it gives them very little room to reconsider.
(Dana lays the notebook flat.)
Dana: At what point does all this planning become a wedding?
Theo: The moment they stop needing me. Naturally, I want them fully prepared.
Dana: What are you calling the package?
Theo: Uncommitted bliss.
Dana: And they’re happy with the package?
Theo: Very. They’ve both started going on dates with other people.
Dana: That sounds like they’ve broken up.
Theo: They still come to every meeting. We’re just being thorough.
Dana: About whether they want to marry each other?
Theo: We compared eighty venues. It seemed odd to stop at the first fiancé.
(Dana looks away.)
Dana: What do you call that part of the service?
Theo: Open auditions.
(On screen: "THREE YEARS." Then: "STILL PLANNING.")
