# Writing for Inkline

What a writer hands in, and what they don't have to think about.

## Hand in plain dialogue

```
Dana: How many paying customers do you have?
Theo: Across all four companies?
Dana: Yes. In total.
(Long pause. Theo keeps smiling.)
Theo: Should we meditate again?
(Both close their eyes. The phone chimes.)
Theo: That's five.
```

- One line per speech, `Name: words`.
- Stage directions in parentheses on their own line. Say what happens, not how long it takes.
- A title at the top. Nothing else.

## Leave these to the pipeline

- Timing: pauses, holds, how long a silence runs.
- Cameras, faces, hands, blinks, breathing.
- Captions, sound effects, the title card.

Stage 01 turns the dialogue into the engine's block format and sets all of that. The reviewer can change any of it before the voices are made.

## Write inside the room

Theo is the guest: a different person every episode who happens to be drawn the same way. Give him any job and say which in the title. Dana is always the same journalist. A script shot in the loft with them can use:
- Two people in two chairs, a low table, a laptop and a phone on it.
- Faces: eyes open, half, closed, one eye open, looking away, looking at the camera (once per video at most).
- Theo's hands: on his knees, palm up, finger up, a small chop, a paste gesture, framing something, hand on heart, hovering over the laptop.
- Dana's hands: pen ready, writing, crossing something out, notebook flat, elbows on knees.
- The phone lighting up with a chime. The laptop screen as its own shot.
- Silent reactions. These are welcome; they are often the funniest part.
- A closing card: end the script with `(On screen: "First line." Then: "Second line.")` and the words appear over the final shot before the fade. Optional.

Anything else (standing up, a third person, another room, a prop that is not on the table) is possible but needs new drawing first. Flag it in the script and it gets costed.

## Tone for this series

- Played straight. Nobody laughs, smirks or winks at the camera.
- The joke is on a habit, never on a real person, company or tool.
- Short lines. One idea per line. The silence after a line does half the work.
